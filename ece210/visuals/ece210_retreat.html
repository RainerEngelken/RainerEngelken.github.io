Interactive ECE 210 visuals

LLMs as rapid prototyping tools

Start by being respectful of the course. ECE 210 already has a strong infrastructure: textbook, mature slides, quizzes, Kahoots, and exam materials. This is a supplementary interactive layer, not a replacement.

The bottleneck

  • the material is strong
  • the textbook is not interactive
  • students struggle to connect
    formalism, intuition, and physical meaning
Suggested phrasing: Students in my sections were often struggling to link the mathematical formalism to conceptual understanding and physical intuition. Drawings only go so far. I wanted visual explanations students could manipulate.

A simple prompt

“Make an awesome web applet based on HTML and Javascript that explains complex numbers for my ECE210 students ‘analog signal processing’. Make it interactive, and pedagogically useful. For context, I am attaching the slides of today’s lecture.”
Say that for simple applets, a short prompt plus attached lecture slides worked surprisingly well. For harder topics, you added more structure: target misconception, required panels, and examples.
slides help a lot4

Workflow

lecture slides + prompt
→ prototype
→ simplify
→ screenshot + rerun if needed
→ audit the math
→ publish as supplementary material
Used: ChatGPT + Gemini
One lesson: asking an LLM to write a super-detailed prompt can work, but often produces overloaded visuals with too many bells and whistles. Simpler was usually better: one concept, a few synchronized views, and few controls.
prompt → prototype → audit5

Caveats

  • not a replacement for study
  • simple beats overloaded
  • polished does not mean correct
  • the instructor keeps intellectual ownership
Mention concrete issues: LaTeX rendering can break; circuit geometry can be wrong; the mathematics can be wrong while the visual looks polished. Also mention intellectual debt: generating fancy visuals without fully understanding what they show.
caveats6

Takeaway

AI helps me build better teaching visuals quickly.

But correctness, pedagogy, and responsibility stay with us.
