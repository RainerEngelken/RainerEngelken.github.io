System Presets

💡 Common Misconceptions:
  • Bounded vs Decaying: A step function $u(t)$ is bounded, but doesn't decay. Integrating it yields a ramp (unbounded).
  • Shift vs Advance: $f(t-2)$ is a delay (causal). $f(t+2)$ looks back in time? No, it needs future inputs!
  • Algebraically Simple ≠ Stable: $y(t) = t \cdot f(t)$ is simple but unstable.
  • $f(t) + 2$ is NOT linear: Linear means $f(t)=0 \implies y(t)=0$.

Concept notes

This applet serves as a conceptual simulator to help students diagnose system properties intuitively and formally.

  • Causality Visualization: When sliding $t_0$, any dependency mapping to $\tau > t_0$ crosses into the visually red "Future" region, explicitly showing causality violations.
  • BIBO Stability Test: Toggling the test signal applies the unit step $u(t)$ (a strictly bounded signal). Students can visually observe if the output $y(t)$ grows unbounded (e.g., in the Accumulator or Time-Varying Gain presets).
  • Time Invariance: Students can use the $t_0$ slider to shift the observation window. For Time-Varying Gain ($t \cdot f(t)$), sliding $t_0$ changes the amplitude of the mapped signal, visually demonstrating shift-variance.
  • Linearity Misconception: The $f(t)+2$ preset proves that $y=mx+b$ logic fails for linear systems (requires zero-in, zero-out).
  • Predictive Pedagogy: Forced choice ("Predict Yes/No") requires cognitive commitment before revealing the answer, improving retention over passive reading.
